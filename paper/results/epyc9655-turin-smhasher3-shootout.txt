READ THESE FIGURES AS INDICATIVE, NOT AS A CROSS-HOST COMPARISON

Four vCPUs, no cpufreq control, no dedicated cores, and a virtualized TSC
whose relationship to the actual core clock is not controlled here. Absolute
values are not comparable with the bare-metal M1 and Zen 5 records. Ratios
between hashes measured on this host in one session are what to read.

Precision is good: after the overhead correction the replicate spread is at
most 0.04 cycles on the small-key column and 0.21 bytes/cycle on bulk. The
numbers are repeatable; their calibration against real core cycles is not
established.

Small-key figures are corrected for the once-per-process overhead
calibration, which ranged 2.29..3.44 cycles here; baseline used 2.29. The
small-key column is dependent latency over 1..31-byte keys, not throughput.

WHAT THIS HOST CONFIRMS

It reproduces, on different silicon and two compiler generations back, the
two findings the bare-metal Zen 5 box produced.

hayahash's bulk lead over ChibiHash v2 is 1.99x here (21.3 against 10.7).
On the bare-metal Zen 5 box it is also 1.99x (31.2 against 15.7). Different
absolute scale, same ratio.

hayahash is ahead of rapidhash v3 in bulk here too, 21.3 against 19.8, the
same ordering the Zen 5 box shows at 31.2 against 28.6. XXH3-64 remains
ahead of both at 23.7, by a narrower margin than on the Zen 5 box.

And ChibiHash v2 is ahead on small-key latency, 13.5 against 17.7, matching
both other x86 hosts. That split is now seen on three separate x86 machines.

THE VECTORIZATION GATE, MEASURED ON A SECOND MACHINE

This host reaches all three shapes, so the AVX-512 dependency can be measured
here rather than inferred:

  gcc -march=native   (tiers 1, vecgcc 1)   small 17.7 cy   bulk 21.3 B/cy
  gcc -U__AVX512DQ__  (tiers 1, vecgcc 0)   small 17.7 cy   bulk 11.8 B/cy
  clang++             (tiers 0, vecgcc 0)   small 17.6 cy   bulk 21.3 B/cy

Vectorized bulk is 1.81x the unvectorized rate. The bare-metal Zen 5 box gives
1.77x for the same comparison. Clang again reaches the vectorized rate without
the special spelling, so the constraint is the instruction set rather than the
compiler, confirmed now on two machines and two compiler generations.
