OVERHEAD CALIBRATION, AND WHY THE RAW NUMBERS NEEDED CORRECTING

SMHasher3 measures the cost of calling a do-nothing hash once per process and
subtracts it from every small-key timing (tests/SpeedTest.cpp, SpeedTestInit).
That single calibration turned out to be noisy: across the 50 runs here it
ranged 1.55 to 3.62 cycles. Because it is subtracted from all 31 key lengths
alike, a bad calibration shifts an entire run by a constant. On this host the
hashes themselves cost 6..18 cycles, so a 2-cycle calibration error is a
10..40% error, and an earlier 3-replicate sweep produced impossible readings
such as 1.59 cycles/hash for a 4-byte rapidhash, below the latency of a single
multiply.

Correction applied here: add each run's own reported overhead back to recover
the raw measurement, then subtract one common baseline for every hash on the
host. The baseline is the smallest calibration observed, 1.55 cycles, since
interference can only inflate the do-nothing hash's measured cost, never push
it below its true value. After correction the replicate spread is at most 0.07
cycles, against +-2.08 before.

Bulk figures are uncorrected: they use a separate long-hash overhead that is
negligible against a 262144-byte hash, and they were already stable.

WHAT THE SMALL-KEY COLUMN MEASURES

timehash_small() feeds each hash's output back into the key between iterations
(j = i ^ hash_temp[0]; memcpy(key, &j, 4)), so hashes cannot overlap. The
small-key column is therefore dependent latency over 1..31-byte keys, not
independent throughput, and it is not comparable to the independent-hash
figures produced by tests/bench.c.
