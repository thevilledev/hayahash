\title{Hayahash: A Portable Scalar Non-Cryptographic Hash\\
with 64- and 128-bit Digests}
\author{Ville Vesilehto}
\date{Working draft, \SnapshotDate}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Hayahash is a seeded non-cryptographic hash function for targets that have
ordinary 64-bit multiplication but no cheap \(64\times64\to128\) product and
no guaranteed SIMD.  This paper is a specification and a structural
analysis.  It states both digest widths and the streaming interface exactly,
and then proves the properties the construction is built on: the absorb
chain is a bijection on stripe sequences, so no difference pattern cancels
inside the absorb; the tail injections and all three finalizers are
bijections; inputs of equal state and unequal length separate in the
finalizer; and on inputs of at most 16 bytes the 128-bit digest is injective
in the message for each fixed length and seed, and injective in the seed for
each fixed message.  Three further lemmas locate the cancellation channels
that the rotation constants close: multiplication modulo \(2^{64}\) fixes a
four-element top-bit subgroup pointwise, a carry-matched difference ladder
under absorb rotation \(r\) returns to those top bit positions only after
\(\min\{d>0 : rd \equiv 0, \pm1 \bmod 64\}\) stripes, and a fold rotation
\(r'\) with \(r+r'\equiv 0 \pmod{64}\) realigns a stripe difference with its
own rotated copy.  A cost model derived from the dependency graph, not from
measurement, shows the bulk loop is bounded by multiplier issue rather than
by latency.  The low word of the 128-bit digest equals the 64-bit digest for
every input and seed, and the streaming interface reproduces both widths
under every split of the input.  There is no proof for the complete
construction and no cryptographic claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The primitive-class constraint}
\label{sec:motivation}

The fastest current 64-bit hashes buy their speed with one of two hardware
assumptions: a cheap \(64\times64\to128\)-bit multiplication folded into the
state, as in the wyhash line \cite{wang2021wyhash,nicoshev2025rapidhash}, or
SIMD widths negotiated per target, as in the fast paths of the xxHash family
\cite{collet2023xxhashspec}.  Both assumptions fail on real targets.
Baseline WebAssembly has \texttt{i64.mul} but neither a 128-bit product nor
guaranteed SIMD; portable-C fallbacks, small in-order cores, and older 64-bit
processors are in the same position.

A second constraint is independent of hardware.  A hash deployed across many
language runtimes must produce one exact digest everywhere, so every required
primitive must exist with identical semantics in every port.  Unsigned 64-bit
addition, XOR, rotation, shifts, and the low half of multiplication meet that
bar; widening multiplies, carry flags, and vector permutes do not.

Hayahash is an answer to the resulting question: what construction is
available when the primitive set is exactly \(\{+, \oplus, \ll, \gg, \rotl,
\times \bmod 2^{64}\}\) on 64-bit words, reads are alignment-safe, and the
result is independent of host byte order?  The interesting part is not that
such a hash exists but which cancellation channels open once the wide product
is removed, and what closes them.  Section~\ref{sec:properties} is that
analysis.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item An exact specification of both digest widths, the streaming interface,
      and the fixed algorithm parameters (Section~\ref{sec:spec}).
\item Bijectivity results for the absorb chain, the injections, and the
      finalizers, and a length-separation lemma that makes the overlapping
      tail reads safe (Sections~\ref{sec:bijectivity}--\ref{sec:length}).
\item Injectivity of the short-input 128-bit digest in the message and,
      separately, in the seed; as a corollary it is a permutation of the
      128-bit space at length 16 (Section~\ref{sec:short128}).
\item A characterization of the difference-cancellation channels specific to
      this primitive class -- top-bit invariance under odd multiplication,
      ladder return distance under the absorb rotation, and fold-rotation
      resonance -- and the exact constant choices that close them
      (Section~\ref{sec:channels}).
\item A dependency-graph cost model that predicts the bulk regime from
      instruction latencies alone (Section~\ref{sec:cost}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope and non-goals}

Hayahash maps a byte string and a 64-bit seed to a 64-bit or 128-bit result,
for hash tables, in-process checksums, and similar non-adversarial uses.  It
is not for passwords, signatures, message authentication, untrusted
hash-table keys, or any use requiring collision or preimage resistance; the
seed is an input, not a security mechanism.  The algorithm is experimental:
constants, thresholds, and digests may change, and nothing should persist
Hayahash values yet.

The design constraints are: unsigned 64-bit scalar arithmetic; multiplication
modulo \(2^{64}\) without the high product; no required SIMD or
architecture-specific extension; alignment-safe reads independent of host
byte order; and one reference mapping across all implementations.  The C
source contains per-target scheduling choices; they use the same operations
and are specified to preserve the digest.

The normative artifact is the C header \cite{vesilehto2026hayahash} as
released in \texttt{\SnapshotRelease}.  It is identified by its SHA-256
digest, not by a commit: any commit that leaves the header byte-identical
carries every result in this paper forward.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

MurmurHash3 established multiply-and-rotate scalar mixing with a strong
bijective finalizer \cite{appleby2011murmurhash3}; Hayahash reuses its
finalizer constants in the 128-bit output path, and the short-input finalizer
is Evensen's \texttt{moremur} refinement of the same shape
\cite{evensen2018moremur}.  wyhash and rapidhash define the frontier where a
widening multiplication is cheap \cite{wang2021wyhash,nicoshev2025rapidhash};
the overlapping-tail read pattern is taken from wyhash.  The xxHash family
documents the scalar and vectorized state of the art
\cite{collet2023xxhashspec}.  ChibiHash v2 occupies the same narrow primitive
class -- a small portable scalar function on the low half of multiplication
-- and is therefore the reference point whenever a comparison is between
design choices rather than instruction sets \cite{nrk2024chibihash}; its
1-to-3-byte load pattern is also borrowed.

Empirical suites and mathematical guarantees are separate kinds of evidence.
SMHasher3 tests distribution and collisions on generated key sets
\cite{wojcik2022smhasher3}; a clean run does not prove collision resistance,
and an earlier iteration of this construction passed the suite while a
constructed collision existed.  PolymurHash states an almost-universality
bound under an explicit model \cite{peters2023polymurhash}.  Hayahash has no
comparable bound; producing one, or documenting why this construction admits
none, is the main open problem (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Notation, constants, interface}
\label{sec:contract}

Let \(\W = \{0,1\}^{64}\), identified with \(\mathbb{Z}/2^{64}\).  Addition,
multiplication, and left shift discard bits above bit~63; right shift is
logical; \(\rotl(x,r)\) rotates left by \(r\).  Bit \(i\) has numeric weight
\(2^i\).  \(\load(B,p)\) reads eight bytes of \(B\) at offset \(p\), first
byte least significant, and \(\loadd\) reads four, zero-extended.  The
implementation realizes loads with \texttt{memcpy} plus byte swaps on known
big-endian hosts, or explicit byte assembly, so no alignment or endianness
assumption reaches the digest.

The input length \(n\) is non-negative with \(n\) readable bytes; a null
pointer is permitted only when \(n=0\).  The constants, all odd, are

\begin{align*}
K   &= \mathtt{9E3779B97F4A7C15}_{16} = \lfloor 2^{64}/\varphi \rfloor, &
M_1 &= \mathtt{3C79AC492BA7B653}_{16}, &
M_2 &= \mathtt{1C69B3F74AC4AE35}_{16},\\
N_1 &= \mathtt{FF51AFD7ED558CCD}_{16}, &
N_2 &= \mathtt{C4CEB9FE1A85EC53}_{16}. & &
\end{align*}

\(M_1, M_2\) are \texttt{moremur} \cite{evensen2018moremur} and \(N_1, N_2\)
the MurmurHash3 finalizer constants \cite{appleby2011murmurhash3}.  Only one
arithmetic property of \(K\) is load-bearing:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kmod4}
K \equiv 1 \pmod 4 .
\end{equation}

Two linear word injections and three finalizers are used:

\begin{align*}
\inj(w)  &= w \oplus \rotl(w,21) \oplus \rotl(w,41), &
\injb(w) &= w \oplus \rotl(w,11) \oplus \rotl(w,50).
\end{align*}

\begin{lstlisting}
fmix(x):                fmix128(x):             lfmix(x):
    x ^= x >> 27            x ^= x >> 30            x ^= x >> 37
    x *= M1                 x *= N1                 x *= K
    x ^= x >> 33            x ^= x >> 31            x ^= x >> 32
    x *= M2                 x *= N2                 return x
    x ^= x >> 27            x ^= x >> 33
    return x                return x
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Specification}
\label{sec:spec}

Every path begins with
\[
    s = \mathit{seed} \oplus K, \qquad \mathit{lenmix} = nK .
\]
The length appears nowhere else: the state depends on the seed alone, and
\(\mathit{lenmix}\) is consumed by the finalizer.  Inputs of at most 16 bytes
take the short path; 17 to 319 bytes run four lanes over 32-byte blocks; 320
bytes and up run eight lanes over 64-byte blocks and then fall through to the
four-lane machinery for the remainder.

\subsection{Inputs of at most 16 bytes}
\label{sec:spec-short}

Two words are formed without reading outside the input:

\begin{lstlisting}
if n >= 8:   a = load64le(B, 0)    b = load64le(B, n - 8)
else if n >= 4:  a = load32le(B, 0)    b = load32le(B, n - 4)
else if n > 0:   a = B[0]              b = (B[n / 2] << 8) | (B[n - 1] << 16)
else:            a = 0                 b = 0
\end{lstlisting}

The head and end reads overlap for some lengths; Lemma~\ref{lem:length} is
what makes that safe.  The words are spread by different injections, combined
with different seed derivations, and multiplied by different odd constants:

\begin{align*}
x &= (\inj(a) \oplus s \oplus K)\,K, &
y &= (\injb(b) \oplus \rotl(s,23) \oplus (K \mathbin{\gg} 19))\,M_1, &
u &= \rotl(x,27) \oplus y \oplus \mathit{lenmix}.
\end{align*}

The 64-bit result is \(\fmix(u)\).

\subsection{The absorb chain}
\label{sec:spec-absorb}

Longer inputs are consumed as a sequence of 64-bit words \(w_0, w_1, \ldots\)
called \emph{stripes}.  With \(w_p\) the previous stripe, initially zero, one
stripe is absorbed into a lane \(h\) as

\begin{lstlisting}
absorb(h, w):   h = (h ^ (w + rotl(wp, 27))) * K ;  wp = w
\end{lstlisting}

The addition happens before the XOR with the lane.  The previous stripe
carries across lane boundaries, block boundaries, and the boundary between
the eight-lane and four-lane loops.  Write \(t_i = w_i + \rotl(w_{i-1},27)\)
for the absorbed value of stripe \(i\), and \(r = 27\) for the absorb
rotation.

\subsection{Inputs of 17 to 319 bytes}
\label{sec:spec-mid}

The four-lane state derives from \(s\) and shifted copies of \(K\):

\begin{align*}
h_0 &= s \oplus K, &
h_1 &= \rotl(s,17) + (K \mathbin{\ll} 21),\\
h_2 &= \rotl(s,34) \oplus (K \mathbin{\gg} 13), &
h_3 &= \rotl(s,51) + (K \mathbin{\ll} 42).
\end{align*}

Each complete 32-byte block absorbs four consecutive stripes into
\(h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3\) in that order.  The remainder \(\rho < 32\) goes to the
wall and tail.

\subsection{Inputs of at least 320 bytes}
\label{sec:spec-bulk}

Four more lanes are initialized:

\begin{align*}
h_4 &= s + (K \mathbin{\gg} 27), &
h_5 &= \rotl(s,13) \oplus (K \mathbin{\ll} 9),\\
h_6 &= \rotl(s,26) + (K \mathbin{\gg} 40), &
h_7 &= \rotl(s,39) \oplus (K \mathbin{\ll} 30).
\end{align*}

Each complete 64-byte block absorbs eight consecutive stripes into
\(h_0,\ldots,h_7\); after the eighth absorb the raw eighth stripe is added to
\(h_0\),
\[
    h_0 \mathrel{+}= w_p ,
\]
which is the \emph{checkpoint} and is part of the digest.  After the last
complete block the upper lanes fold into the lower:

\begin{align*}
h_0 &\leftarrow (h_0 \oplus \rotl(h_4,11))\,K, &
h_1 &\leftarrow (h_1 \oplus \rotl(h_5,19))\,K,\\
h_2 &\leftarrow (h_2 \oplus \rotl(h_6,31))\,K, &
h_3 &\leftarrow (h_3 \oplus \rotl(h_7,47))\,K.
\end{align*}

If at least 32 bytes remain, one four-lane block runs; the previous-stripe
chain continues across the fold.

\subsection{Wall, tail, and finalizer}
\label{sec:spec-tail}

After the complete-block loops the final stripe's dangling rotated copy is
absorbed.  This is the \emph{wall}; it is a no-op when no complete stripe was
processed:
\[
    h_0 \mathrel{+}= \rotl(w_p,r).
\]

With \(p\) the offset of the first unconsumed byte and \(\rho < 32\) the
remaining length, the tail absorbs whole words through \(\inj\):

\begin{lstlisting}
if rho > 16:   h0 = (h0 + inj(load64le(B, p + 0))) * K
               h1 = (h1 + inj(load64le(B, p + 8))) * K
if rho >  0:   h2 = (h2 + inj(load64le(B, n - 16))) * K
               h3 = (h3 + inj(load64le(B, n -  8))) * K
\end{lstlisting}

The last two reads always cover the final 16 bytes of the input and may
overlap bytes already absorbed; at \(\rho = 0\) there are no tail reads.  The
finalizer folds the lanes, absorbs the length inside the first
multiplication, and avalanches:

\begin{align*}
t_0 &= (h_0 \oplus \rotl(h_1,13) \oplus \mathit{lenmix})\,K, &
t_1 &= (h_2 \oplus \rotl(h_3,33))\,K, &
z   &= s \oplus t_0 \oplus \rotl(t_1,29).
\end{align*}

The 64-bit result is \(\lfmix(z)\).

\subsection{The 128-bit digest}
\label{sec:spec-128}

hayahash128 runs the identical state walk and extracts two words at the end.
On the short path, with \(u\) as in Section~\ref{sec:spec-short}, and on the
longer paths with \(t_0,t_1\) as above:

\begin{align*}
\mathit{lo} &= \fmix(u), &
\mathit{hi} &= \fmixw(x + \rotl(u,32)) & &(n \leq 16),\\
\mathit{lo} &= \lfmix(z), &
\mathit{hi} &= \fmixw(\rotl(s,32) \oplus (t_1 + \rotl(t_0,47))) & &(n \geq 17).
\end{align*}

The low word is exactly hayahash64 for every input and seed.  The high word
combines the fold words with addition where the low word uses XOR, so one
GF(2)-linear cancellation cannot erase a difference from both words, and it
is finalized by a different bijection.

\subsection{Fixed algorithm parameters}
\label{sec:spec-params}

Three values are algorithm parameters rather than tuning knobs: the bulk
threshold \(320\), the absorb rotation \(r=27\), and the block sizes
\(64\) and \(32\).  An input of exactly 320 bytes takes the eight-lane path.
Moving the threshold changes every digest at and around it and, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:stripes}, can invalidate the stripe bound the rotation
argument depends on.  Dispatch shape -- straight-line length tiers, loop
unrolling, whether the bulk loop is outlined, whether a compiler vectorizes
it -- is chosen per architecture and compiler and is specified to leave the
digest unchanged.

\subsection{The streaming interface}
\label{sec:spec-streaming}

The streaming state holds the eight lanes, the previous stripe, the seed, the
running total, and a 448-byte buffer with a 128-byte retention floor.
\texttt{update} appends bytes; \texttt{digest} may be called at any time,
does not modify the state, and returns either width.  Bytes are consumed only
in whole 64-byte blocks, in one-shot block order from stream offset zero; at
digest time the buffer retains at least 128 trailing bytes, which contains
every address the tail reads; totals below 448 bytes remain fully buffered
and dispatch to the one-shot function.  Proposition~\ref{prop:streaming}
states the resulting guarantee.

\section{Structural properties}
\label{sec:properties}

Arithmetic is modulo \(2^{64}\) throughout.  Sections~\ref{sec:bijectivity}
to \ref{sec:short128} establish what the construction preserves;
Section~\ref{sec:channels} characterizes what it must destroy, and
Section~\ref{sec:notproved} states the boundary.

\subsection{Bijectivity}
\label{sec:bijectivity}

\begin{lemma}[Absorb chain bijectivity]
\label{lem:chain}
Fix \(w_{-1} \in \W\) and \(m \geq 0\), and define
\(\Phi(w_0,\ldots,w_m) = (t_0,\ldots,t_m)\) with
\(t_i = w_i + \rotl(w_{i-1},r)\).  Then \(\Phi\) is a bijection on
\(\W^{m+1}\), and if two stripe sequences first differ at index \(j\), their
images agree before index \(j\) and differ at index \(j\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Given \((t_0,\ldots,t_m)\), recover \(w_0 = t_0 - \rotl(w_{-1},r)\) and
\(w_i = t_i - \rotl(w_{i-1},r)\) in order, so \(\Phi\) is invertible.  If the
sequences agree before index \(j\), the images agree there and the values
\(w_{j-1}\) coincide, so \(t_j\) differs exactly when \(w_j\) differs,
because adding a fixed word is injective.
\end{proof}

Two distinct inputs that disagree in some stripe therefore feed the lane
machinery different absorb values at the \emph{first} differing stripe.  The
lemma says nothing about what the lane recurrence and folds do afterward;
that is the subject of Section~\ref{sec:channels}.  Note also what fails
without the chain: a stripe transform of the form
\(t_i = \bigoplus_k w_{i-k}\!\ll\! c_k\) is GF(2)-linear in the stripe
sequence and, once the sum runs over more than one index, has a nonzero
kernel, so difference patterns exist that vanish in every absorbed value.

\begin{lemma}[Injection bijectivity]
\label{lem:inj}
For \(0 < a < b < 64\), the map
\(\iota(w) = w \oplus \rotl(w,a) \oplus \rotl(w,b)\) is a bijection on \(\W\);
in particular \(\inj\) and \(\injb\) are.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\(\iota\) is GF(2)-linear and commutes with rotation, so it lies in the ring
\(\mathbb{F}_2[z]/(z^{64}+1)\), with \(\iota\) corresponding to
\(p(z) = 1 + z^a + z^b\).  Over \(\mathbb{F}_2\) we have
\(z^{64}+1 = (z+1)^{64}\), so the ring is local with maximal ideal
\((z+1)\), and \(p\) is a unit if and only if \(p(1) \neq 0\).  With an odd
number of terms \(p(1) = 1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The same argument shows any odd number of rotation terms is invertible and
any even number is not: an even-term \(p\) has \(p(1)=0\), hence lies in the
maximal ideal and annihilates the all-ones word.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Finalizer bijectivity]
\label{lem:fmix}
The maps \(x \mapsto x \oplus (x \mathbin{\gg} k)\) for \(1 \leq k \leq 63\)
and \(x \mapsto cx\) for odd \(c\) are bijections on \(\W\); hence
\(\fmix\), \(\fmixw\), and \(\lfmix\) are bijections.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the shift map the top \(k\) bits of the image equal those of the
argument, and each lower bit is the argument bit XOR an already-recovered
higher bit, so the argument is recovered from the top down.  An odd \(c\) is
a unit modulo \(2^{64}\).  The finalizers are compositions of these maps.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Length separation}
\label{sec:length}

\begin{lemma}[Length separation]
\label{lem:length}
Fix a seed, and let two inputs have lengths \(n \neq n'\) with
\(0 \leq n, n' < 2^{63}\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item If both lengths are at most 16 and the inputs load equal word pairs
      \((a,b)\), the 64-bit digests differ, and the 128-bit digests differ in
      the low word.
\item If both lengths are at least 17 and the inputs reach the finalizer with
      equal lane values \((h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3)\), the 64-bit digests differ, and
      the 128-bit digests differ in both words.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\(K\) is odd, so \(n \mapsto nK\) is injective on \(\mathbb{Z}/2^{64}\), and
lengths below \(2^{63}\) are distinct as words: \(\mathit{lenmix} \neq
\mathit{lenmix}'\).

Case 1.  Equal \((a,b)\) and seed give equal \((x,y)\), so \(u\) and \(u'\)
differ, and \(\fmix\) is a bijection (Lemma~\ref{lem:fmix}).

Case 2.  Equal lane values give equal \(h_0 \oplus \rotl(h_1,13)\), so the
pre-multiplication values of \(t_0\) and \(t_0'\) differ and multiplication
by odd \(K\) is injective: \(t_0 \neq t_0'\), while \(t_1 = t_1'\).  The
low-word input \(s \oplus t_0 \oplus \rotl(t_1,29)\) then differs and
\(\lfmix\) is a bijection.  The high-word input \(\rotl(s,32) \oplus (t_1 +
\rotl(t_0,47))\) differs because rotation and addition of the fixed \(t_1\)
are injective, and \(\fmixw\) is a bijection.
\end{proof}

The lemma covers exactly the collision class the overlapping reads create.
Distinct-length inputs can produce identical loaded words and identical
absorb sequences -- all-zero inputs of lengths 17 through 31 are the extreme
case, since the tail reads then cover the same zero bytes -- and within each
path family such pairs never collide.  The placement of
\(\mathit{lenmix}\) is what carries the proof: it enters \emph{inside} the
\(t_0\) multiplication, so the difference \(nK - n'K\) is spread by \(K\)
before reaching the finalizer.  XOR-ing \(nK\) after the final
multiplications would still separate the two inputs, but the resulting
difference lies in a low-order subgroup fixed by the remaining operations,
which is detectable as modular structure across lengths.

\subsection{Short-input injectivity}
\label{sec:short128}

\begin{theorem}[Short-input 128-bit injectivity]
\label{thm:short128}
Fix a seed and a length \(n \leq 16\).  The map from \(n\)-byte inputs to
\((\mathit{lo},\mathit{hi})\) is injective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The map factors as \(B \mapsto (a,b) \mapsto (x,y) \mapsto (u,x) \mapsto
(\mathit{lo},\mathit{hi})\).

\((a,b)\) determines \(B\).  For \(8 \leq n \leq 16\), \(a\) holds bytes
\(0..7\) and \(b\) holds bytes \(n-8..n-1\) with \(n-8 \leq 8\), so every
input byte occupies a fixed position of \(a\) or \(b\); for \(4 \leq n \leq
7\) the same holds with four-byte words; for \(1 \leq n \leq 3\), \(a =
B[0]\) and \(b\) holds \(B[\lfloor n/2 \rfloor]\) and \(B[n-1]\) in disjoint
bit ranges, and the positions \(0\), \(\lfloor n/2 \rfloor\), \(n-1\) cover
every index below \(n\).  Distinct equal-length inputs therefore differ in a
fixed bit of \((a,b)\).

\((a,b) \mapsto (x,y)\) is a bijection for fixed seed: each coordinate
composes a bijective injection (Lemma~\ref{lem:inj}), an XOR with a constant,
and an odd multiplication (Lemma~\ref{lem:fmix}).  \((x,y) \mapsto (u,x)\) is
a bijection for fixed length, since \(y = u \oplus \rotl(x,27) \oplus
\mathit{lenmix}\).  Finally \((u,x) \mapsto (\fmix(u), \fmixw(x +
\rotl(u,32)))\) is a bijection: recover \(u\) from \(\mathit{lo}\), then
\(x\) from \(\mathit{hi}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:perm16}
For each seed, hayahash128 restricted to inputs of exactly 16 bytes is a
permutation of the 128-bit space.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Seed injectivity on the short path]
\label{cor:seed}
Fix a length \(n \leq 16\) and an input \(B\).  The map
\(\mathit{seed} \mapsto (\mathit{lo},\mathit{hi})\) is injective.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The last two factors of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:short128} recover
\((u,x)\) from \((\mathit{lo},\mathit{hi})\) for fixed \(n\).  With \(B\)
fixed, \(a\) is fixed and \(x = (\inj(a) \oplus s \oplus K)K\) determines
\(s = xK^{-1} \oplus \inj(a) \oplus K\), and \(\mathit{seed} = s \oplus K\).
\end{proof}

No analogous claim holds for the 64-bit digest alone, nor across distinct
lengths, nor above 16 bytes; a 64-bit digest of arbitrarily long inputs
necessarily has collisions.  Corollary~\ref{cor:seed} is the exact sense in
which the short path cannot lose the seed: an adversary choosing key bits
cannot erase the seed from the pair, because the pair determines it.

\subsection{Cancellation channels}
\label{sec:channels}

The lemmas above say differences enter the lanes.  This section says where
they could leave.  Three channels exist in this primitive class, and each is
closed by an explicit constant choice.

\begin{lemma}[Top-window invariance]
\label{lem:top}
Let \(c\) be odd and \(U = \{0,\, 2^{62},\, 2^{63},\, 2^{62}+2^{63}\}
\subset \W\), the subgroup generated by \(2^{62}\).  Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(c \cdot 2^{63} = 2^{63}\) for every odd \(c\), and
\item \(U\) is fixed pointwise by \(\delta \mapsto c\delta\) if and only if
      \(c \equiv 1 \pmod 4\).
\end{enumerate}
In particular, by \eqref{eq:kmod4}, multiplication by \(K\) is the identity
on \(U\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\(c \cdot 2^{63} \equiv 2^{63} c \equiv 2^{63} \pmod{2^{64}}\) because
\(2^{63}(c-1) \equiv 0\) whenever \(c\) is odd.  For \(2^{62}\),
\(c \cdot 2^{62} - 2^{62} = 2^{62}(c-1)\), which vanishes modulo \(2^{64}\)
exactly when \(4 \mid c-1\).  Both generators fixed implies all of \(U\)
fixed, since the map is additive.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:top} is the reason an XOR-linked absorb is not enough.  A
difference planted in the top window survives every multiplication by \(K\)
unchanged, so it can be cancelled later by an equal difference introduced at
any subsequent stripe of the same lane: multiplication spreads bits only
upward and does nothing at all here.  The chained rotated copy is the
countermeasure.  Because \(t_i = w_i + \rotl(w_{i-1},r)\), a difference in
stripe \(i-1\) also appears \emph{rotated} in \(t_i\), at position
\(q + r \bmod 64\) rather than \(q\), and combined by addition rather than
XOR.  Cancelling both copies at once requires the carry pattern of an
addition to agree with an XOR mask, which is a condition on values, not on
structure.

The residual of such a carry-matched cancellation walks by \(r\) positions
per stripe.  The next lemma bounds how quickly it can return to the only
place where it becomes free again.

\begin{lemma}[Ladder return distance]
\label{lem:ladder}
Let \(r\) be the absorb rotation, and consider a single-bit residual at
position \(q\) that is displaced to \(q + r \bmod 64\) at each subsequent
stripe.  If \(\gcd(r,64)=1\) the residual visits every bit position with
period 64.  The least \(d>0\) for which some \(q \in \{62,63\}\)
satisfies \(q + rd \bmod 64 \in \{62,63\}\) is
\[
  D(r) \;=\; \min\{\, d>0 \;:\; rd \equiv 0, 1, \text{ or } -1 \pmod{64} \,\}.
\]
Then \(D(21) = 3\) and \(D(27) = 19\).  Moreover, a residual that must
re-enter the top window \emph{in the same lane} of an \(L\)-lane loop is
restricted to \(d \in L\mathbb{Z}\), so its reachable displacements form the
subgroup \(\langle Lr \rangle = \gcd(Lr,64)\,\mathbb{Z}/64\); at \(r=27\)
this subgroup has order 8 for \(L=8\) and order 16 for \(L=4\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\(\gcd(r,64)=1\) makes \(r\) a unit modulo 64, so \(d \mapsto rd\) is a
bijection of \(\mathbb{Z}/64\) and the walk has period 64.  From \(q=63\),
re-entry needs \(rd \equiv 0\) or \(rd \equiv -1\); from \(q=62\), \(rd
\equiv 0\) or \(rd \equiv 1\); \(D(r)\) is the minimum over both.  For
\(r=21\): \(21 \cdot 3 = 63 \equiv -1\), and \(21d \bmod 64 \notin
\{0,1,63\}\) for \(d \in \{1,2\}\).  For \(r=27\): \(27 \cdot 19 = 513
\equiv 1\), and enumerating \(27d \bmod 64\) for \(d = 1,\ldots,18\) gives
\(27,54,17,44,7,34,61,24,51,14,41,4,31,58,21,48,11,38\), none of which lies
in \(\{0,1,63\}\).  The same-lane statement is the observation that stripe
indices of one lane differ by multiples of \(L\), and the set
\(\{Lrd \bmod 64 : d \in \mathbb{Z}\}\) is the cyclic subgroup generated by
\(Lr\), of order \(64/\gcd(Lr,64)\).
\end{proof}

A ladder is a chain of such cancellations, one per stripe, each paid for with
carry luck; it terminates in an exact collision only if the residual is back
in the multiplication-invariant window when its lane closes.  \(D(27) = 19\)
against \(D(21) = 3\) is a factor of six in the number of independent carry
conditions a construction must satisfy, and the rotation is \(27\) for that
reason.  Two further mechanisms bound the same family from the other side.

\begin{proposition}[Stripe bounds]
\label{prop:stripes}
In the four-lane loop the absorb chain never exceeds
\(4\lfloor 319/32 \rfloor = 36 < 64\) chained stripes: for \(n \leq 319\) the
loop runs at most \(\lfloor 319/32 \rfloor = 9\) blocks, and for \(n \geq
320\) it runs at most one block after the bulk loop.  In the eight-lane loop
the checkpoint \(h_0 \mathrel{+}= w_p\) injects a raw, unmultiplied stripe
into lane \(0\) every 8 stripes.  The wall \(h_0 \mathrel{+}= \rotl(w_p,r)\)
injects the final stripe's dangling rotated copy on every path that
processes at least one complete stripe.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from the dispatch conditions of Section~\ref{sec:spec}: the
four-lane loop consumes 32 bytes per iteration and runs only while at least
32 bytes remain, with entry length below \(320\) on the mid path and below
\(64\) on the bulk path.
\end{proof}

The three statements compose.  A residual under \(r=27\) needs 19 stripes to
return to the top window at all and \(d \in L\mathbb{Z}\) to return in its own
lane; the four-lane path is 36 stripes long, one full rotation orbit is 64,
and in the eight-lane path a raw stripe enters lane 0 every 8 stripes, so an
orbit that would otherwise hide a difference until it returns to its starting
lane is interrupted before it gets there.  The wall closes the remaining
escape: without it, the rotated copy of the \emph{last} stripe is never
absorbed, so a ladder can be terminated by placing its final difference past
the end of the input.

The last channel is in the fold rather than the absorb.

\begin{lemma}[Fold-rotation resonance]
\label{lem:fold}
\(\rotl(\cdot,r')\) inverts \(\rotl(\cdot,r)\) if and only if
\(r + r' \equiv 0 \pmod{64}\).  For \(r = 27\) that is \(r' = 37\); the
finalizer uses \(r' = 33\), and \(27 + 33 = 60 \not\equiv 0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\(\rotl(\rotl(w,r),r') = \rotl(w,r+r' \bmod 64)\), which is the identity
exactly when \(r+r' \equiv 0\).
\end{proof}

The finalizer computes \(t_1 = (h_2 \oplus \rotl(h_3,r'))K\).  Lanes 2 and 3
are adjacent, so a stripe difference \(\delta\) in lane 2 appears in lane 3
as \(\rotl(\delta,r)\) through the chained copy.  With \(r' = 37\) the fold
would rotate it back to \(\delta\) and align the two occurrences for XOR
cancellation -- a structural cancellation, not a carry-dependent one.
Choosing \(r'=33\) removes the alignment.  The remaining fold rotations
(\(13\) in \(t_0\), \(29\) on \(t_1\), \(11,19,31,47\) in the eight-to-four
fold) are likewise not congruent to \(-r\).

\begin{remark}[Why the folds multiply]
Each fold has the shape \((h \oplus \rotl(h',\cdot))K\) rather than
\(h + \rotl(h',\cdot)\).  The absorb combines with addition; an additive fold
would put the difference and its copy in the same algebra, where exact
cancellation is a linear condition.  Mixing XOR into a chain of additions
means cancellation again requires a carry pattern rather than an identity.
The same reasoning explains the high word of the 128-bit digest: it combines
\(t_0\) and \(t_1\) additively where the low word uses XOR, so a single
GF(2)-linear relation cannot annihilate both.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Streaming equivalence}
\label{sec:streaming}

\begin{proposition}[Streaming equivalence]
\label{prop:streaming}
For every seed, byte sequence, and partition of the sequence into update
calls, \texttt{hayahash64\_digest} equals \texttt{hayahash64} of the whole
sequence, and \texttt{hayahash128\_digest} equals \texttt{hayahash128}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The state after \(k\) bytes is a function of the seed and those \(k\) bytes
alone, because every lane initial value derives from \(s\) and the length
enters only at the finalizer.  Totals below the 448-byte buffer capacity stay
contiguous in the buffer and dispatch to the one-shot function directly.
Otherwise the total exceeds 320 bytes, so the one-shot function would take
the eight-lane path and the state machine has committed to the same path.
Updates consume input only in complete 64-byte blocks, in stream order from
offset zero, through the identical absorb-and-checkpoint transform, matching
the one-shot path, which consumes complete blocks while at least 64 bytes
remain.  At digest time the buffered remainder replays the one-shot epilogue
on a copy of the lanes: leftover blocks, fold, at most one 32-byte round,
wall, tail, and finalizer with \(\mathit{lenmix}\) from the running total.
The 128-byte retention floor guarantees the buffered suffix contains the last
16 bytes of the stream, the deepest address the tail reads back.  The
operation sequences are identical and every read returns the same byte.
\end{proof}

A differential test checks this mechanically: every length through 512 under
three seeds and five split patterns, plus 1{,}000 randomized cases through
20~KiB, requiring one-shot and streaming equality for both widths and
\(\mathtt{hayahash128.lo} = \mathtt{hayahash64}\).

\subsection{What is not proved}
\label{sec:notproved}

These results are local.  They do not bound collisions of the complete
construction.  The lane recurrence
\(h \leftarrow (h \oplus t)K\) alternates a GF(2)-linear operation with a
\(\mathbb{Z}/2^{64}\)-linear one; the two do not commute and the composite
has no closed form in either algebra, which is exactly why the construction
resists structural analysis and equally why no bound is offered here.  The
folds and the 64-bit truncation are outside the scope of every statement
above.  Hayahash has no almost-universality bound and no adversarial
guarantee of any kind.  The value of Section~\ref{sec:properties} is
negative-space: it closes the enumerated structural channels and delimits
where only empirical evidence exists.

\section{Cost model}
\label{sec:cost}

The bulk loop's shape follows from a dependency-graph argument that needs no
measurement.  Let a block-structured absorb have \(L\) independent lanes over
a block of \(B\) bytes with one multiplication per 8-byte stripe.  Write
\(\lambda_\times\) and \(\lambda_+\) for multiplication and single-cycle ALU
latencies, \(\mu\) for the sustained multiplication issue rate in multiplies
per cycle, and \(c\) for the longest loop-carried dependency chain in any
lane, measured in cycles per block.

\begin{proposition}[Bulk rate bounds]
\label{prop:cost}
In steady state the loop sustains at most
\[
  \min\!\left(\frac{B}{c},\; 8\mu\right) \quad\text{bytes per cycle},
\]
the first term from the carried chain and the second from multiplier issue.
The loop is issue-bound when \(B/c \ge 8\mu\) and latency-bound otherwise.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each lane's state at block \(m+1\) depends on its state at block \(m\)
through a chain of length \(c\), so no lane can advance faster than one block
per \(c\) cycles; the \(L\) lanes are independent, so the block rate is
\(1/c\) and the byte rate \(B/c\).  Each 8-byte stripe requires one
multiplication, so a rate of \(\beta\) bytes per cycle demands
\(\beta/8 \le \mu\).
\end{proof}

For Hayahash, \(B = 64\), \(L = 8\).  Lanes \(1\ldots7\) carry
\(h \leftarrow (h \oplus t)K\), a chain of length
\(\lambda_\oplus + \lambda_\times\); lane 0 additionally carries the
checkpoint add, giving \(2\lambda_+ + \lambda_\times\).  The absorb value
\(t = w + \rotl(w_p,r)\) is computed from loaded words and is not on any
carried chain.  With the common \(\lambda_+ = \lambda_\oplus = 1\),
\(\lambda_\times = 3\) the two chains are 4 and 5, so \(c = 5\) and
\(B/c = 12.8\) bytes per cycle, against \(8\mu = 8\) at \(\mu = 1\).  The loop is issue-bound with a factor
\(12.8/8 = 1.6\) of latency headroom, and stays issue-bound for any
\(\mu \le 1.6\).  On AArch64 the checkpoint folds into a multiply-add and
\(c_0\) drops toward 4.

The comparison with ChibiHash v2 is instructive because the two functions do
the \emph{same amount of work} per stripe.  Both spend five operations per
8-byte stripe: Hayahash a load, a rotate, an add, an XOR, and a multiply;
ChibiHash v2 a load, an add, a multiply, a rotate, and a cross-lane add.  The
difference is placement.  ChibiHash v2 has \(B = 32\), \(L = 4\), and its
carried chain per lane is the cross-lane add, the stripe add, and the
multiply, so \(c = 2\lambda_+ + \lambda_\times = 5\) and
\(B/c = 6.4 < 8\mu\): latency-bound, at 80\% of what its own multiplier issue
would allow.  Hayahash reaches the issue bound with identical instruction
density purely by keeping the cross-stripe coupling off the carried path --
the rotated copy is taken from a register already loaded, and the lanes never
touch each other inside the loop.

\begin{remark}
This is a first-order model.  It ignores load-port limits, front-end issue
width for the four non-multiply operations per stripe, and reorder-window
effects at block boundaries, and it assumes the lanes fit in registers, which
is what motivates the eight-lane bound rather than a wider state.  It
predicts the \emph{regime} -- which resource binds -- not a rate.  Measured
throughput on specific hosts is recorded in the repository, deliberately
outside this paper.
\end{remark}

\section{Limitations and open problems}
\label{sec:limitations}

Standing limitations: the algorithm is experimental and digests may change;
there is no cryptographic or adversarial claim of any kind; the structural
results do not bound the complete construction; the harness and SMHasher3 are
statistical evidence only; the streaming state buffers up to 448 bytes.

Open problems, in decreasing order of interest:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A bound for the lane recurrence.}  Model
      \(h \leftarrow (h \oplus t)K\) over the mixed algebra of
      Section~\ref{sec:notproved} and derive either an almost-universality
      bound comparable in form to PolymurHash's
      \cite{peters2023polymurhash} or a documented argument that this
      construction admits none.
\item \textbf{Quantifying the ladder cost.}  Lemma~\ref{lem:ladder} counts
      stripes between top-window returns but does not bound the probability
      that the required carry patterns are simultaneously satisfiable.  A
      bound on the number of free carry conditions per ladder step would turn
      a design heuristic into a theorem.
\item \textbf{An archived differential search.}  A rerunnable search over the
      absorb chain and folds, replacing the ad-hoc constructions that found
      the channels of Section~\ref{sec:channels}.
\item \textbf{Multi-host SMHasher3 coverage.}  Archived raw full-suite runs
      for both widths across the compiled dispatch shapes.
\item \textbf{One generated machine-readable vector table} consumed by every
      port, replacing the per-port tables.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

Removing the wide product from a hash's primitive set does not merely cost
throughput; it opens specific algebraic channels, because multiplication
modulo \(2^{64}\) is the only nonlinear operation left and it is the identity
on a four-element subgroup at the top of the word.  Hayahash closes those
channels with a chained absorb that is bijective on stripe sequences
(Lemma~\ref{lem:chain}), a rotated copy that forces cancellation to depend on
carries rather than structure (Lemma~\ref{lem:top}), rotation constants
chosen so that a ladder cannot return to the invariant window quickly
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:ladder} and \ref{lem:fold}), and a length term positioned so
that overlapping tail reads remain separated (Lemma~\ref{lem:length}).  The
same state walk yields a 128-bit digest whose low word is the 64-bit digest
and which is injective on short inputs in both the message and the seed, and
a streaming interface that reproduces both widths exactly.  The cost model of
Section~\ref{sec:cost} shows that this can be done while remaining bound by
multiplier issue rather than by dependency latency.  What remains open is a
bound for the complete construction; until it exists, the empirical evidence
justifies continued work and nothing stronger.
