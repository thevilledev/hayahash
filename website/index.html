hayahash64

A 64-bit non-cryptographic hash function that passes the full SMHasher3 suite while staying strictly portable: no SIMD, no 64×64→128-bit multiply, no per-architecture code, no undefined behavior, and endianness-independent output. The reference implementation is one C99 header, hayahash.h, with bit-exact ports to nine other targets. Haya (速) is Japanese for "fast".

It is for platforms where only ordinary 64-bit multiplication is available: WebAssembly, the JVM, .NET, and portable C. If you are on native x86-64 or ARM64 and can use a wide multiply, rapidhash v3 is still the better default. That trade-off is measured, not hidden; see the benchmarks.

Experimental. The algorithm, constants, and digest values may still change. Do not use hayahash where hash values are persisted, exchanged between systems, or used for security. It is not a cryptographic hash.

Benchmarks

Two claims, both scoped and both reproducible:

  1. Among hashes restricted to portable scalar 64-bit code, hayahash has the fastest sustained bulk rate that passes the complete SMHasher3 suite, on both machines measured.
  2. On Zen 5, where the compiler auto-vectorizes its bulk loop, it also streams faster than every wide-multiply hash measured, rapidhash v3 included. Only XXH3-64 and gxhash are faster there, and both fail the suite.

The small-key column is dependent latency (each output feeds the next key), so it is not comparable to independent-hash throughput. Our own runs at a pinned SMHasher3 commit, five replicates per cell, measured at v0.4.0. Full tables, hosts, method, and caveats: benchmarks.

The closest comparable design is ChibiHash — same portability rules, and v2 also passes the suite. Head-to-head at 1 MiB, hayahash streams 30.2 vs 18.8 GB/s on the M1 (1.6×) and 61.3 vs 31.2 GB/s on Zen 5 (2.0× with AVX-512 auto-vectorization; about 35 GB/s without). Small keys split by architecture: hayahash takes 11% less time on the M1, 30% more on Zen 5.

Quality

  • SMHasher3: 188/188 tests, verification 0xF3C4A9B4, re-verified at v0.4.0 on nine builds spanning four hosts, five compilers, and all three dispatch shapes the header compiles. ChibiHash v2 also passes; v1 fails.
  • Local harness: strict avalanche criterion over input and seed bits, plus exact-collision tests over 24 structured key sets, including reproductions of the SMHasher3 keysets that broke earlier iterations of this design.
  • All nine ports are bit-exact against the C reference: shared known-answer vectors and the verification value in every port's test suite, plus nightly differential fuzzing that replays a fresh randomized C-reference corpus through every language port.
  • Endianness and ABI are tested in CI: big-endian s390x, ILP32 wasm32, MSVC x64, and MIPS64 under qemu must all reproduce the shared vectors.

Details and reproduction steps: quality.md; claim-by-claim evidence in AUDIT.md.

Design, in four lines

  1. Bulk loop: 8 independent lanes over 64-byte blocks, nothing longer than add → mul on the loop-carried path.
  2. Each lane absorbs w + rotl(w_prev, 27) — injective by first-difference induction.
  3. All lane constants are derived from one premixed seed+length value; no big per-lane literals.
  4. Tails read whole overlapping words, wyhash-style; inputs of at most 16 bytes take a dedicated two-multiply path.

The hard part was closing every structural collision class SMHasher3 found in earlier iterations without giving the speed back. More on both: design.
